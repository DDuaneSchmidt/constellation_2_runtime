# HOSTILE_REVIEW_001

## Scope

Implementation reference: IMP_001 — SPY 200DMA Trend Following

Evidence source: BACKTEST_001

This hostile review attacks the evidence generated by BACKTEST_001. It does not certify IMP_001, does not certify CLAIM_001, and does not recommend capital allocation.

## Hostile Questions

| Question | Classification | Review |
|---|---|---|
| Is performance improvement merely lower exposure? | CONCERN | IMP_001 was in market 71.35% of the time and had lower volatility than buy-and-hold. It beat the 50/50 SPY/cash baseline on CAGR and Sharpe, so the result is not explained solely by lower exposure, but exposure reduction is a major contributor to the drawdown profile. |
| Is any benefit concentrated in one period? | CONCERN | The clearest benefits occur in 2000-2003 and 2008. The 2020 and validation-period comparisons show weaker return capture versus buy-and-hold. Benefits are not purely one-period, but bear-market avoidance is central to the evidence. |
| Does validation-period performance survive? | CONCERN | Validation-period CAGR was positive at 10.75% and Sharpe was 0.935, slightly above buy-and-hold Sharpe. However, validation CAGR lagged buy-and-hold materially, and drawdown/ulcer results were worse than the 50/50 baseline. |
| Does performance survive higher costs? | PASS | At 20 bps round-trip, CAGR was 6.46% and Sharpe was 0.629. At 50 bps round-trip, CAGR was 5.47% and Sharpe was 0.543. Higher costs degrade but do not erase the evidence. |
| Does performance survive execution delay? | PASS | The 1-day execution-delay stress reported CAGR of 7.05%, Sharpe of 0.674, and max drawdown of -24.32%, broadly similar to the base case. |
| Do 180DMA and 220DMA perturbations behave similarly? | PASS | 220DMA was close to base case. 180DMA remained positive but weaker, with CAGR of 5.90%, Sharpe of 0.580, and max drawdown of -29.73%. Nearby parameters are directionally similar, though 180DMA is a weaker variant. |
| Does IMP_001 beat the random timing distribution? | CONCERN | IMP_001 ranked at 100% on random CAGR and Sharpe. Raw random drawdowns were much worse than IMP_001. The reported max-drawdown percentile rank is 0.00%, which appears directionally inconsistent with the raw drawdown distribution and requires artifact review before relying on that field. |
| Are drawdown improvements meaningful? | PASS | Full-period max drawdown improved from -55.20% for buy-and-hold to -24.45% for IMP_001. The 2008 drawdown reduction was especially large. |
| Are return reductions acceptable? | CONCERN | Full-period CAGR declined from 8.35% for buy-and-hold to 6.79% for IMP_001. Validation-period CAGR declined from 15.03% for buy-and-hold to 10.75% for IMP_001. Whether this is acceptable depends on the intended use case and cannot be decided by this factory. |
| Is this result deployable for David? | CONCERN | Rules are simple, auditable, and low turnover enough for practical deployment, but deployment review must consider tax burden, brokerage execution, operator workflow, and whether lower return capture is acceptable. This factory does not allocate capital. |

## Hostile Findings

1. IMP_001 provides evidence of a deployable implementation with meaningful drawdown reduction versus buy-and-hold.
2. IMP_001 does not dominate buy-and-hold because it gives up full-period and validation-period CAGR.
3. IMP_001 does not dominate the 50/50 baseline because validation-period drawdown and ulcer results are worse than that lower-exposure baseline.
4. Evidence is materially dependent on SPY and one implementation, so it cannot certify CLAIM_001.
5. Random baseline drawdown percentile reporting should be reviewed before any implementation decision relies on that specific percentile field.

## Impact

Claim impact: weak positive

Implementation impact: mixed

The evidence is weak positive for the broader claim only in the sense that one reasonable implementation produced non-random, stress-resistant evidence. It is not sufficient to certify CLAIM_001.
