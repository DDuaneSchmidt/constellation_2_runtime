# Claim 001 Validation Plan

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the research program required to validate or falsify CLAIM_001.

The plan assumes CLAIM_001 is false until evidence demonstrates otherwise. It does not perform historical research, populate case studies, or create conclusions. It defines the procedure, evidence standards, controls, and decision rules required before any claim of validation is allowed.

## 2. Claim Statement

CLAIM_001:

A significant percentage of exceptional long-duration investment outcomes can be explained by a limited set of recurring predictive opportunity archetypes.

## 3. Why Claim 001 Matters

CLAIM_001 is foundational to the AEGIS Investment Thesis Factory. If recurring predictive archetypes do not exist, then the factory cannot rely on archetype discovery as a systematic research advantage.

If the claim fails, the factory must treat exceptional outcomes as mostly idiosyncratic, reduce reliance on archetype-based reasoning, and avoid converting retrospective explanations into discovery machinery.

## 4. Null Hypothesis

The validation program begins with this null hypothesis:

- Exceptional investment outcomes are primarily unique.
- Recurring predictive archetypes do not exist.
- Observed patterns are largely hindsight artifacts.

The burden of proof is on CLAIM_001, not on the null hypothesis.

## 5. Success Criteria

CLAIM_001 can only be supported if evidence demonstrates that:

- Archetypes recur across multiple industries.
- Archetypes are visible before outcomes become obvious.
- Archetypes demonstrate predictive usefulness.
- Archetypes discriminate between stronger and weaker opportunities.
- A limited archetype set explains a meaningful percentage of cases.

Success requires cross-case evidence, not isolated examples.

## 6. Failure Criteria

CLAIM_001 fails if evidence shows:

- No recurring archetypes identified.
- Archetypes require hindsight.
- Archetypes fail predictive usefulness tests.
- Archetypes fail discrimination tests.
- Archetype count becomes too large to be operationally useful.
- Historical winners cannot be classified consistently.

Failure must be recorded directly. It must not be softened through post-hoc redefinition.

## 7. Research Questions

The research program must answer:

- Do recurring predictive archetypes exist?
- How many archetypes are required?
- Do archetypes generalize across industries?
- Do archetypes identify opportunities prospectively?
- Do archetypes improve discovery quality?

Each question must be answered with evidence and contradiction review.

## 8. Dataset Construction Rules

Target:

- 50-100 exceptional historical investment outcomes.

Requirements:

- Multiple industries.
- Multiple decades.
- Multiple economic environments.
- Multiple business models.

The dataset must be constructed before scoring begins. Case inclusion rules must be documented to avoid selecting examples that fit preferred archetypes.

## 9. Historical Case Selection Rules

Cases should be selected based on:

- Exceptional long-duration investment outcomes.
- Not popularity.
- Not familiarity.
- Not ease of analysis.

Selection must avoid using well-known narratives as a proxy for evidence quality. A case is eligible because of the outcome profile, not because it is easy to explain.

## 10. Counterexample Selection Rules

The validation set must include:

- Historical failures.
- False positives.
- Businesses that appeared similar but did not produce exceptional outcomes.

Counterexamples are required to test whether archetypes discriminate or merely describe winners after the fact.

## 11. Validation Procedure

The validation procedure must use:

- `01_discovery_validation_standard.md`
- `02_historical_case_template.md`
- `03_archetype_validation_protocol.md`

Procedure:

- Build the case universe and counterexample set.
- Define discovery dates for each case.
- Apply information-boundary rules.
- Evaluate present realities, market beliefs, underappreciation, consequence chains, value capture, contradiction, and alternatives.
- Classify archetypes only when evidence supports classification.
- Score case-level and cross-case evidence.
- Decide whether CLAIM_001 is validated, partially validated, inconclusive, or rejected.

## 12. Evidence Requirements

The validation program requires:

- Case-level evidence.
- Cross-case evidence.
- Counterexample evidence.
- Contradiction evidence.

Evidence must be time-bounded to the relevant discovery date. Later outcomes may define case eligibility, but they must not be used to infer what was knowable before the outcome became obvious.

## 13. Archetype Identification Rules

Rules:

- Do not force classifications.
- Allow no-archetype outcomes.
- Allow multiple archetypes.
- Require evidence for every classification.

An archetype label is invalid unless the underlying pattern was observable before broad recognition and can be stated without relying on the eventual outcome.

## 14. Predictive Usefulness Assessment

For each archetype classification, answer:

- Could the archetype have been identified at the time?
- Would it have generated a useful hypothesis?
- Would it have survived contradiction review?

An archetype that cannot generate a useful pre-outcome hypothesis does not support CLAIM_001.

## 15. Discrimination Assessment

For each archetype, answer:

- Does the archetype separate exceptional outcomes from non-exceptional outcomes?
- Does it narrow the discovery search space?
- Does it improve discovery efficiency?

Discrimination must be tested against failures, false positives, and failed analogs. Broad labels with weak selectivity do not support the claim.

## 16. Survivorship Bias Controls

Mandatory controls:

- Include losers.
- Include false positives.
- Include failed analogs.

The validation program must not infer archetype validity from winners alone. Cases where similar features failed are required evidence.

## 17. Hindsight Bias Controls

Mandatory controls:

- Only information available at the discovery date may be used.
- No retrospective narratives.
- No outcome-dependent reasoning.

If a classification depends on later earnings, later stock performance, subsequent management choices, or retrospective narrative coherence, it is invalid for CLAIM_001.

## 18. Confidence Scoring

Score each dimension from 1 to 10:

- Recurrence.
- Predictive Utility.
- Discrimination.
- Generalizability.
- Evidence Quality.
- Overall Confidence.

Every score must include evidence, counterevidence, and confidence notes. Unsupported scores are invalid.

## 19. Decision Framework

Possible outcomes:

- Validated.
- Partially Validated.
- Inconclusive.
- Rejected.

Decision rules:

- Validated: Strong evidence supports recurrence, predictive utility, discrimination, generalizability, and evidence quality.
- Partially Validated: Evidence supports a narrowed version of the claim, but material uncertainty remains.
- Inconclusive: Evidence is insufficient, inconsistent, or too weak to accept or reject the claim.
- Rejected: Evidence supports the null hypothesis or shows that archetypes fail predictive, discriminatory, or hindsight controls.

## 20. Outputs

Required outputs:

- Claim 001 Evidence Report.
- Validated Archetype Registry.
- Rejected Archetype Registry.
- Confidence Scorecard.
- Recommended Factory Changes.

Outputs must distinguish evidence from interpretation and must not present conclusions beyond the validation result.
