# Discovery Candidate Definition Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines what qualifies as a Discovery Candidate within the AEGIS Investment Thesis Factory.

The goal is to prevent the factory from producing observations, narratives, trends, or opinions that do not meet the threshold for investment-thesis discovery. A Discovery Candidate is not accepted because it is interesting, plausible, timely, or well-written. It is assumed invalid until evidence accumulates.

## 2. Why Discovery Candidates Matter

Discovery Candidates are the atomic unit of the Investment Thesis Factory. If candidate quality is weak, later thesis construction, consequence mapping, and investment evaluation will inherit unsupported assumptions.

The candidate definition must therefore separate evidence-based discovery from:

- General market commentary.
- Retrospective explanation.
- Opinionated narrative.
- Thematic speculation.
- Security selection.
- Portfolio action.

The factory can only be evaluated if candidates are defined consistently, documented rigorously, and rejected aggressively when evidence is insufficient.

## 3. Formal Definition

A Discovery Candidate is:

```text
A potentially important,
true,
underappreciated,
investable reality
whose implications may not be fully appreciated.
```

Each term is required. A candidate that fails any element is not a valid Discovery Candidate.

## 4. Non-Goals

Discovery Candidates are not:

- Stock recommendations.
- Trade ideas.
- Valuation opinions.
- Portfolio decisions.
- Technical signals.
- Narratives without evidence.
- Macroeconomic forecasts.

Discovery Candidates identify realities for validation. They do not prescribe investment action.

## 5. Required Characteristics

A valid Discovery Candidate must be:

- Observable.
- Evidence-supported.
- Economically relevant.
- Falsifiable.
- Potentially investable.
- Capable of contradiction review.

Failure to meet any required characteristic is sufficient reason for rejection.

## 6. Four-Filter Validation Model

Every Discovery Candidate must be evaluated against four filters.

### Truth

Assessment question: Is the proposed reality likely true?

Required review:

- Evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Confidence Assessment.

### Importance

Assessment question: Could the reality matter economically?

Required review:

- Evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Confidence Assessment.

### Underappreciation

Assessment question: Are the implications insufficiently recognized by relevant market participants?

Required review:

- Evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Confidence Assessment.

### Investability

Assessment question: Could the reality plausibly connect to an investable consequence chain?

Required review:

- Evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Confidence Assessment.

## 7. Discovery Candidate Lifecycle

Discovery Candidates must be tracked using one of the following lifecycle states:

- Candidate.
- Under Review.
- Supported Candidate.
- Rejected Candidate.
- Promoted Candidate.
- Retired Candidate.

Lifecycle state changes must be documented. Promotion is not permanent; later evidence may require degradation, retirement, or rejection.

## 8. Discovery Candidate Evidence Requirements

Every Discovery Candidate must document:

- Observable facts.
- Supporting evidence.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Alternative explanations.
- Evidence quality assessment.

Evidence must be specific enough for independent review. Unsupported claims, inferred motives, and narrative coherence are not substitutes for evidence.

## 9. Discovery Candidate Scoring Framework

Each Discovery Candidate must be scored from 1 to 10 across the following dimensions:

- Truth.
- Importance.
- Underappreciation.
- Investability.
- Evidence Quality.
- Consequence Depth.
- Overall Confidence.

Scores must be justified in writing. A high score without supporting rationale is invalid.

## 10. Discovery Candidate Failure Modes

Common failure modes include:

- True but unimportant.
- Important but obvious.
- Important but not investable.
- Narrative-driven.
- Forecast-dependent.
- Cannot be falsified.
- No consequence chain.
- No value capture path.

Failure modes must be treated as evidence against candidate validity, not as refinements to be explained away.

## 11. Discovery Candidate Rejection Rules

Reject a Discovery Candidate if it:

- Fails any Four-Filter category.
- Cannot be supported with evidence.
- Cannot survive contradiction review.
- Requires hindsight to appear compelling.

Rejection is a normal output of the factory. A high rejection rate is acceptable if it improves discovery discipline.

## 12. Discovery Candidate Promotion Rules

Promote a Discovery Candidate only if:

- All four filters pass.
- Evidence exceeds contradiction.
- Economic relevance is established.
- Potential value capture is identified.

Promotion means the candidate is eligible for further thesis development. It does not mean the candidate is an investment recommendation.

## 13. Contradiction Review Requirements

Contradiction review is mandatory. Every Discovery Candidate must answer:

- What evidence argues against the candidate?
- What alternative explanation exists?
- What evidence would invalidate the candidate?
- What assumptions are being made?

Candidates without documented contradiction review are incomplete and cannot be promoted.

## 14. Documentation Requirements

Every Discovery Candidate must include:

- Candidate ID.
- Discovery Date.
- Description.
- Evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Scoring.
- Status.
- Reviewer Notes.

Documentation must preserve the reasoning available at the time of discovery. Later outcomes must not be used to improve the apparent quality of the original candidate.

## 15. Governance Rules

- No candidate is accepted by intuition.
- No candidate is accepted by narrative quality.
- Evidence is required.
- Contradictions must be documented.
- Discovery and investment evaluation remain separate stages.

Governance must maintain a strict distinction between identifying a potentially important reality and deciding whether any security should be bought, sold, or held.

## 16. Outputs

This standard supports production of:

- Discovery Candidate Registry.
- Candidate Scorecards.
- Promotion Decisions.
- Rejection Decisions.
- Discovery Quality Reports.

Outputs must make candidate status, evidence quality, and rejection rationale auditable.
