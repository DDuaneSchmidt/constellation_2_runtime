# Discovery Candidate Rejection Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines how Discovery Candidates are formally rejected within the AEGIS Investment Thesis Factory.

Rejection is a research output. It preserves learning, prevents repeated weak investigation, and protects the factory from narrative accumulation. Rejected candidates must remain visible and auditable.

## 2. Rejection Philosophy

Rejection is not failure of the factory.

Rejection is evidence discipline.

Rejection is how weak candidates are removed from active consideration without losing the learning that came from review.

Every candidate remains assumed invalid unless it survives evidence review, contradiction review, alternative explanation review, and scoring governance.

## 3. Rejection Scope

This standard applies when a candidate cannot remain active under the current evidence state.

Rejection may occur from:

- CANDIDATE.
- UNDER_REVIEW.
- SUPPORTED_CANDIDATE.
- PROMOTED_CANDIDATE.

Rejection means the candidate is considered invalid or insufficiently supported under current governance. It is distinct from retirement.

## 4. Non-Goals

Rejection must not be used to:

- Hide poor research quality.
- Delete inconvenient evidence.
- Improve apparent hit rate.
- Remove contradiction history.
- Rewrite candidate lineage.
- Avoid reviewer accountability.

Rejected candidates remain part of the research record.

## 5. Required Rejection Preservation

Every rejection must preserve:

- Failed criteria.
- Contradictions.
- Alternative explanations.
- Evidence weaknesses.
- Lessons learned.

These records must be specific enough for later reviewers to understand why the candidate failed.

## 6. Failed Criteria Requirement

The rejection record must identify which criteria failed.

Failed criteria may include:

- Truth.
- Importance.
- Underappreciation.
- Investability.
- Evidence quality.
- Consequence depth.
- Value capture plausibility.
- DQI sufficiency.
- Review completion.

The rejection decision must identify whether failure was decisive or cumulative.

## 7. Contradiction Preservation Requirement

Contradictions must remain visible after rejection.

The rejection record must preserve:

- Contradictory evidence.
- Contradiction severity.
- Affected claims.
- Reviewer interpretation.
- Impact on scores.

Contradictions must not be deleted because the candidate is no longer active.

## 8. Alternative Explanation Requirement

Rejected candidates must preserve alternative explanations reviewed during rejection.

The record must answer:

- What other explanations fit the evidence?
- Why did those explanations weaken the candidate?
- Which explanation was preferred, if any?
- What uncertainty remains?

Alternative explanations are part of the learning record and must remain visible.

## 9. Evidence Weakness Requirement

The rejection record must identify evidence weaknesses.

Evidence weaknesses may include:

- Insufficient source quality.
- Stale evidence.
- Incomplete evidence.
- Assumption-heavy reasoning.
- Unsupported claims.
- Weak causality.
- Low falsifiability.
- Hindsight dependence.

Evidence weakness must be documented directly, not hidden behind general rejection language.

## 10. Lessons Learned Requirement

Every rejection must preserve lessons learned.

Lessons may include:

- Weak discovery pattern.
- Repeated evidence gap.
- Common false positive signal.
- Broken consequence chain.
- Overstated underappreciation.
- Unsupported investability.

Lessons learned exist to improve future candidate creation and review discipline.

## 11. Rejected Candidate Archive

Rejected candidates must remain in a Rejected Candidate Archive.

The archive must preserve:

- Candidate ID.
- Candidate title.
- Rejection date.
- Rejection reason.
- Failed criteria.
- Evidence references.
- Contradiction references.
- Alternative explanations.
- Score history.
- Review history.
- Lessons learned.
- Reopening conditions, if any.

The archive is part of the factory's evidence memory. It must not be treated as disposal.

## 12. Visibility Rules

Rejected candidates must remain visible to future reviewers.

Rules:

- No deletion.
- No silent removal.
- No hidden rejection.
- No overwriting rejected candidates.
- No ID reuse.
- No removal from lineage.

Visibility is required so future research does not unknowingly recreate rejected claims.

## 13. Explicit Rejection Reasons

Every rejection requires explicit rejection reasons.

Acceptable reason categories include:

- Failed truth filter.
- Failed importance filter.
- Failed underappreciation filter.
- Failed investability filter.
- Evidence quality insufficient.
- Contradiction exceeds evidence.
- Alternative explanation stronger.
- Consequence chain unsupported.
- Value capture path unsupported.
- DQI insufficient.
- Review protocol incomplete.

Generic reasons such as "not compelling" or "low priority" are insufficient.

## 14. Rejection Audit Trail

Every rejection requires an audit trail that preserves:

- Decision date.
- Prior status.
- New status.
- Reviewer identity or role.
- Review inputs.
- Failed criteria.
- Evidence basis.
- Contradictions.
- Score changes.
- Final rationale.

The audit trail must allow later reviewers to reconstruct the decision without relying on memory.

## 15. Rejection From Promoted Candidate

Promoted candidates may be rejected when later evidence invalidates the basis for promotion.

The rejection record must compare:

- Original promotion rationale.
- New contradictory evidence.
- Score degradation.
- Failed assumptions.
- Impact on consequence chain.
- Impact on value capture path.

Prior promotion does not protect a candidate from rejection.

## 16. Reopening Rejected Candidates

Rejected candidates may only be reopened under explicit governance.

Reopening requires:

- New evidence.
- Reopening rationale.
- Preservation of original rejection record.
- Review of whether candidate meaning changed.
- New ID if the candidate meaning materially changed.

No rejected candidate may be silently restored to active status.

## 17. Governance Rules

- No deletion of rejected candidates.
- No silent removal.
- No rejection without explicit rationale.
- No rejection without preserving contradictions.
- No rejection without lessons learned.
- No reopening without governance.
- No ID reuse.

Rejection governance must treat failed candidates as evidence assets.

## 18. Outputs

This standard supports production of:

- Rejection Decision Record.
- Rejection Audit Trail.
- Rejected Candidate Archive.
- Lessons Learned Record.
- Updated Status History.

Outputs must preserve research learning and support future hostile review.
