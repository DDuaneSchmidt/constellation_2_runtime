# Archetype Validation Protocol

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines the methodology required to determine whether recurring predictive investment archetypes actually exist and whether they can be identified before outcomes become obvious.

All archetypes are unproven. The objective is not to validate archetypes. The objective is to falsify archetypes and retire weak explanations before they become research doctrine.

## 2. Core Question

Do recurring predictive investment archetypes exist that could have been identified before outcomes became obvious?

## 3. Validation Philosophy

Archetype validation must follow these principles:

- Archetypes are assumed false until supported by evidence.
- Explanatory power is insufficient.
- Predictive usefulness is required.
- Historical repetition alone is insufficient.
- Archetypes must demonstrate discriminatory value.

An archetype that explains known winners after the fact but cannot identify candidates before recognition is not predictive. It is descriptive at best.

## 4. Definitions

- Descriptive Archetype: A recurring pattern that can organize historical cases but has not demonstrated prospective usefulness or discriminatory value.
- Predictive Archetype: A recurring pattern that can generate useful hypotheses before outcomes become obvious and can distinguish stronger candidates from weaker candidates.
- Validated Archetype: A predictive archetype that has passed recurrence, discrimination, predictive usefulness, evidence quality, and survivorship-bias controls.
- Retired Archetype: An archetype removed from active use because it fails discrimination tests, fails predictive usefulness tests, requires hindsight, or cannot be applied prospectively.
- Archetype Candidate: A proposed recurring pattern submitted for validation and treated as false until evidence supports promotion.

## 5. Archetype Candidate Registry

Initial Archetype Candidate Registry:

- Persistence Mispricing
- Economic Capture Mispricing
- Reinvestment Mispricing
- Expectation Shock Mispricing
- Structural Change Mispricing
- Misclassification
- Constraint Persistence

Registry membership does not imply validity.

The registry is a queue of claims to test, not a list of accepted patterns.

## 6. Evidence Standards

Each Archetype Candidate must produce evidence for:

- Historical recurrence.
- Prospective usefulness.
- Cross-industry applicability.
- Non-trivial explanatory value.
- Ability to identify opportunities before outcomes become obvious.

Evidence must be time-bounded, falsifiable, and linked to specific cases. Unsupported narrative similarity is not evidence.

## 7. Historical Validation Procedure

Historical validation must use `02_historical_case_template.md`.

Minimum target:

- 50 historical cases.

The case set must include successes, failures, false positives, weak candidates, and cases where the archetype appears plausible but does not produce useful consequence chains.

No hindsight evidence is allowed. Historical validation must define what was knowable at the discovery date and exclude future outcomes, later earnings results, later stock performance, subsequent management decisions, and retrospective narrative construction.

## 8. Predictive Usefulness Test

Each Archetype Candidate must answer:

- Could the archetype have been recognized at the time?
- Would it have generated a useful hypothesis?
- Would the hypothesis have survived contradiction review?

An archetype fails this test if it becomes clear only after the outcome, if it generates vague hypotheses, or if contradiction review eliminates its useful signal.

## 9. Discrimination Test

Each Archetype Candidate must answer:

- Does the archetype identify winners more frequently than random candidates?
- Does the archetype distinguish strong opportunities from weak opportunities?
- Does the archetype narrow the search space?

An archetype must improve selection quality. A broad label that applies to nearly everything has no discriminatory value.

## 10. Survivorship Bias Controls

Validation must include:

- Historical failures.
- False positives.
- Non-winning businesses with similar characteristics.

A candidate archetype cannot be promoted using only successful cases. It must be tested against cases where similar features did not produce meaningful discovery value.

## 11. Hindsight Controls

Every validation report must explicitly review:

- Was the archetype visible before the outcome?
- Or only after the outcome became known?

If the archetype requires outcome knowledge to become coherent, it fails. If the archetype depends on later facts to define its boundaries, it remains unvalidated or is retired.

## 12. Archetype Scoring Framework

Score each dimension from 1 to 10:

- Recurrence.
- Predictive Utility.
- Discrimination.
- Cross-Industry Strength.
- Evidence Quality.
- Falsifiability.

Each score must include written justification, evidence links, counterevidence, and confidence level. Scores without contradiction review are invalid.

## 13. Archetype Promotion Rules

An Archetype Candidate becomes a Validated Archetype only if it demonstrates:

- Strong recurrence.
- Strong discrimination.
- Strong predictive usefulness.
- No major survivorship bias concerns.

Promotion requires evidence that the archetype can be used prospectively. Historical explanation alone is not enough.

## 14. Archetype Degradation Rules

Confidence must be reduced when:

- Counterexamples accumulate.
- Discrimination weakens.
- Predictive usefulness declines.

Degradation must be recorded explicitly. The protocol must not preserve confidence through vague caveats or narrower post-hoc definitions.

## 15. Archetype Retirement Rules

Retire an archetype when it:

- Fails discrimination tests.
- Fails predictive usefulness tests.
- Requires hindsight to function.
- Cannot be applied prospectively.

Retired archetypes may remain in the Rejected Archetype Registry for auditability but must not be used to support discovery claims.

## 16. Failure Conditions

The validation program fails if evidence shows:

- No recurring predictive archetypes found.
- Archetypes explain history but do not predict.
- Archetypes rely heavily on hindsight.
- Archetypes fail to improve discovery quality.
- Archetypes fail to narrow search space.

Failure is a valid research result. It must not be reframed as validation.

## 17. Outputs

Required outputs:

- Validated Archetype Registry.
- Rejected Archetype Registry.
- Archetype Confidence Scores.
- Archetype Evidence Reports.

Each output must distinguish descriptive patterns from predictive archetypes.
