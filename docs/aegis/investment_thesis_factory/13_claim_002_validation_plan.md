# Claim 002 Validation Plan

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the research program required to validate or falsify CLAIM_002.

The plan exists to test the claim, not to defend it. CLAIM_002 must be assumed false until evidence demonstrates otherwise. The research program must be hostile, evidence-first, and designed to expose cases where the claim depends on hindsight, narrative quality, luck, or superior forecasting rather than observable present realities.

This document does not perform historical research, populate case studies, or create conclusions.

## 2. Claim Statement

CLAIM_002:

```text
Important investment opportunities often originate from underappreciated present realities, not superior long-range forecasting.
```

## 3. Why Claim 002 Matters

If CLAIM_002 is true, the Investment Thesis Factory should focus discovery work on observable present realities whose implications are underappreciated, economically important, and potentially investable.

If CLAIM_002 is false, the factory may be overemphasizing present-state discovery and underestimating the role of superior forecasting, luck, case-specific judgment, or non-repeatable insight.

The claim matters because it affects what kind of evidence the factory should prioritize before large-scale evidence collection begins.

## 4. Null Hypothesis

```text
Exceptional investment outcomes primarily depend on superior forecasting, luck, or case-specific factors rather than identifiable present realities.
```

The null hypothesis is the default position. Evidence must overcome it; absence of disproof is not support.

## 5. Success Criteria

CLAIM_002 may be supported only if evidence shows that:

- Present realities were observable before outcomes became obvious.
- Those realities were economically important.
- Their implications were underappreciated.
- The opportunity did not require precise long-range forecasting.
- The reality could plausibly have generated a Discovery Candidate.

Each success criterion must be tested independently. A case that passes some criteria but fails others cannot be treated as full support.

## 6. Failure Criteria

CLAIM_002 must be weakened or rejected when evidence shows that:

- Cases require hindsight to identify the present reality.
- Cases depend primarily on forecasting.
- Present realities were already obvious.
- Present realities were not economically important.
- Present realities do not improve discovery quality.
- Value capture depended on case-specific luck rather than an identifiable reality.
- The proposed reality could not have generated a valid Discovery Candidate at the time.

Failure cases must be preserved, not explained away.

## 7. Research Questions

The validation program must answer:

- Did underappreciated present realities precede exceptional outcomes?
- Were those realities observable at the time?
- Could they have been discovered systematically?
- Did they matter economically?
- Did they reduce reliance on long-range forecasting?

The research must distinguish what was knowable at the time from what became obvious only after outcomes occurred.

## 8. Required Evidence

Validation requires:

- Contemporaneous evidence.
- Case-level evidence.
- Contradictory evidence.
- Alternative explanations.
- Evidence that underappreciation existed.

Evidence must be durable enough for later audit. Interpretation must be separated from source facts. Unsupported claims, retrospective explanations, and narrative coherence are not evidence.

## 9. Historical Case Procedure

Historical cases must be reviewed using:

- `02_historical_case_template.md`.
- `05_case_universe_construction_standard.md`.
- `06_discovery_candidate_definition_standard.md`.

The procedure must define the case universe before judging outcomes. Cases must not be selected only because they make CLAIM_002 appear true.

Each case review must preserve:

- Discovery date.
- Evidence available at discovery date.
- Proposed present reality.
- Contradictions.
- Alternative explanations.
- Forecasting dependence assessment.
- Underappreciation assessment.
- Investment relevance assessment.

## 10. Present Reality Identification Rules

The proposed present reality must be:

- Observable.
- Evidence-supported.
- Economically relevant.
- Not dependent on knowing the future outcome.

The present reality must be stated as something a reviewer could have observed at the discovery date. It must not be reverse-engineered from later success.

Invalid present realities include:

- Claims that require later outcomes to become legible.
- Claims that are only forecasts in present-tense language.
- Claims without contemporaneous evidence.
- Claims whose economic relevance depends entirely on hindsight.

## 11. Forecasting Dependence Test

Each case must answer:

- Could the opportunity have been identified without forecasting the exact future?
- Did the thesis depend on long-range macro, technology, or market predictions?
- Was the key insight an observed reality or a speculative forecast?

The test must classify the role of forecasting as:

- Low dependence.
- Moderate dependence.
- High dependence.
- Required.

High or required forecasting dependence weakens CLAIM_002 unless the present reality independently explains why the opportunity was discoverable before the outcome.

## 12. Underappreciation Test

Each case must answer:

- Was the reality broadly recognized?
- Were its implications broadly recognized?
- Was the market likely misreading the significance?

Underappreciation requires evidence. It cannot be inferred from later price movement or later consensus change alone.

Valid underappreciation evidence may include contemporaneous commentary, coverage gaps, consensus expectations, valuation treatment, strategic neglect, or documented disagreement about implications.

## 13. Investment Relevance Test

Each case must answer:

- Could the reality lead to value creation?
- Could value be captured?
- Could the implication become investment-relevant?

Investment relevance must be evaluated without producing a security recommendation, trade idea, portfolio action, or allocation decision.

The test must distinguish:

- Economic reality.
- Value creation.
- Value capture.
- Investment relevance.

Failure at any link weakens the case for CLAIM_002.

## 14. Counterexample Requirements

The research program must include counterexamples where:

- Present realities existed but did not lead to investment opportunity.
- Present realities were obvious.
- Forecasting was required.
- Value capture failed.

Counterexamples must be treated as first-class evidence. They must be preserved even when they complicate the claim.

The counterexample set must be large enough to test whether the claim improves discovery quality or merely describes selected winners after the fact.

## 15. Hindsight Bias Controls

Rules:

- Discovery date must be established before analysis.
- Only information available at the discovery date may be used.
- Do not use later outcomes to define the present reality.

Additional controls:

- Do not select cases only because outcomes were exceptional.
- Do not allow later terminology to redefine the original reality.
- Do not treat eventual success as proof that the reality was underappreciated.
- Do not upgrade evidence quality after observing the outcome.

Any case that cannot satisfy hindsight controls must be marked as compromised.

## 16. Confidence Scoring

Each case must be scored from 1 to 10 across:

- Present Reality Visibility.
- Economic Importance.
- Underappreciation.
- Forecast Independence.
- Investability.
- Evidence Quality.
- Overall Confidence.

Scores require written rationale. Overall Confidence must not exceed Evidence Quality. Cases with weak contemporaneous evidence, high hindsight dependence, or unresolved contradiction must receive lower confidence.

## 17. Decision Framework

Possible outcomes:

- Validated.
- Partially Validated.
- Inconclusive.
- Rejected.

Validated requires broad evidence that CLAIM_002 explains multiple cases better than forecasting, luck, or case-specific factors.

Partially Validated means the claim appears useful in some cases but fails or remains ambiguous in others.

Inconclusive means evidence is insufficient, mixed, or too compromised by hindsight to support a decision.

Rejected means evidence favors the null hypothesis or shows that CLAIM_002 does not improve discovery quality.

## 18. Outputs

The validation program must produce:

- Claim 002 Evidence Report.
- Present Reality Case Map.
- Forecast Dependence Assessment.
- Counterexample Report.
- Recommended Factory Changes.

Outputs must preserve evidence, contradictions, alternative explanations, scoring, and decision rationale. Recommended Factory Changes must remain research-process changes only and must not create investment recommendations or trading actions.
