# Discovery Candidate Retirement Standard

## 1. Purpose

This standard defines retirement governance for Discovery Candidates that are no longer active but are not necessarily invalid.

Retirement preserves research history while removing a candidate from active review. Retirement is distinct from rejection and must not be used to hide invalid candidates, unresolved contradictions, or incomplete review.

## 2. Retirement Philosophy

Retirement is a state of inactivity, not a claim of invalidity.

A retired candidate may have been useful, plausible, stale, superseded, or no longer relevant to current research priorities. Its evidence and review history must remain preserved.

No candidate may disappear from the research record because it was retired.

## 3. Retirement Scope

Retirement may apply to:

- SUPPORTED_CANDIDATE.
- PROMOTED_CANDIDATE.
- UNDER_REVIEW, when review is explicitly closed without rejection.
- CANDIDATE, when the candidate is preserved but not advanced.

Retirement must not be used when the evidence clearly invalidates the candidate. Invalid candidates require rejection.

## 4. Retirement Versus Rejection

Retirement and rejection are distinct.

Rejection means the candidate failed current governance or evidence standards.

Retirement means the candidate is no longer active but may remain plausible, historically useful, stale, superseded, or outside current research scope.

The decision record must explain why retirement is appropriate instead of rejection.

## 5. Required Retirement Record

Every retirement must document:

- Retirement rationale.
- Retirement date.
- Future reactivation conditions.
- Evidence state.
- Historical scores.

The record must be sufficient for later reviewers to understand why the candidate became inactive.

## 6. Retirement Rationale Requirement

The retirement rationale must explain the specific reason for inactivity.

Acceptable retirement rationales include:

- Candidate superseded by a better-defined candidate.
- Research question no longer active.
- Evidence became stale without decisive rejection.
- Candidate completed its intended research role.
- Candidate remains plausible but no longer warrants active review.
- Candidate was merged into a materially different candidate with lineage preserved.

Vague rationale such as "not needed" or "lower priority" is insufficient.

## 7. Retirement Date Requirement

Every retirement must include a retirement date.

The date must preserve when the candidate left active review. Later reviewers must be able to distinguish evidence available before retirement from evidence that emerged after retirement.

## 8. Future Reactivation Conditions

Every retired candidate must define future reactivation conditions.

Reactivation conditions may include:

- New evidence.
- Material market structure change.
- New contradiction resolution.
- New consequence chain evidence.
- New value capture evidence.
- Updated historical comparison.
- Reviewer decision to reopen under governance.

Retired candidates must not be silently resurrected.

## 9. Evidence State Requirement

The retirement record must document the candidate's evidence state at retirement.

Evidence state must include:

- Supporting evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Alternative explanations.
- Evidence gaps.
- Evidence freshness.
- Evidence quality.

Evidence state must be preserved even if later evidence changes the candidate's interpretation.

## 10. Historical Scores Requirement

Retirement must preserve historical scores.

The score record must include:

- Truth Score.
- Importance Score.
- Underappreciation Score.
- Investability Score.
- Evidence Quality Score.
- Consequence Depth Score.
- Contradiction Strength Score.
- Overall Confidence Score.
- DQI.
- Score rationale.

Scores must not be deleted or overwritten when a candidate is retired.

## 11. Lineage Preservation

Retirement requires preservation of lineage.

The record must show:

- Original candidate ID.
- Related candidates.
- Successor candidates, if any.
- Superseding candidates, if any.
- Prior statuses.
- Material changes.

Lineage must make clear whether a future candidate is a continuation, reopening, successor, or new claim.

## 12. Review History Preservation

Retirement must preserve review history.

Review history must include:

- Review dates.
- Reviewer identity or role.
- Review inputs.
- Findings.
- Status recommendations.
- Score changes.
- Rationale.

Review history must remain available even when the candidate is no longer active.

## 13. Evidence History Preservation

Retirement must preserve evidence history.

Evidence history must include:

- Evidence references.
- Evidence added over time.
- Evidence removed from active consideration.
- Contradictions.
- Alternative explanations.
- Evidence quality assessments.

Evidence history must distinguish evidence from interpretation.

## 14. Score History Preservation

Retirement must preserve score history.

Score history must include:

- Initial scores.
- Updated scores.
- Score degradation.
- Score improvement.
- Score rationale.
- Reviewer calibration changes.

Retirement must not flatten score history into a final score.

## 15. Reopening Governance

Retired candidates may only be reopened under explicit governance.

Reopening requires:

- Reopening rationale.
- Evidence or condition that triggered reopening.
- Review of prior retirement rationale.
- Review of current evidence state.
- Preservation of original retirement record.
- New review protocol completion.

No silent resurrection is allowed.

## 16. Material Change and New IDs

Materially changed candidates require new IDs.

Material changes include:

- Different reality claim.
- Different consequence chain.
- Different value capture path.
- Different investability claim.
- Different underappreciation claim.
- Reframing that changes falsifiability.

The new candidate must link back to the retired candidate as lineage. It must not overwrite or reuse the retired candidate ID.

## 17. Governance Rules

- Retirement is distinct from rejection.
- Retirement requires rationale.
- Retirement requires date.
- Retirement requires reactivation conditions.
- Retirement preserves lineage.
- Retirement preserves review history.
- Retirement preserves evidence history.
- Retirement preserves score history.
- Material changes require new IDs.
- No silent resurrection.

Retirement governance must preserve candidate memory without implying current support.

## 18. Outputs

This standard supports production of:

- Retirement Decision Record.
- Retirement Audit Trail.
- Retired Candidate Record.
- Reopening Review Record.
- Updated Status History.
- Candidate Lineage Record.

Outputs must be durable, auditable, and clear about the difference between inactivity and invalidity.
