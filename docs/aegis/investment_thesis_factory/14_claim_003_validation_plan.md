# Claim 003 Validation Plan

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the research program required to validate or falsify CLAIM_003.

The plan exists to test whether implications of present realities can be traced systematically. It does not assume that consequence tracing works. CLAIM_003 must be treated as false until evidence shows that implication chains can be built, challenged, repeated, and audited without relying on hindsight, narrative quality, analyst confidence, or investment outcomes.

This document does not perform historical research, populate case studies, create Discovery Candidates, create conclusions, or recommend investment action.

## 2. Claim Statement

CLAIM_003:

```text
The implications of present realities can be traced systematically.
```

## 3. Validation Objective

The objective is to determine whether a present reality can be translated into a traceable consequence chain through a disciplined research process.

Validation must test whether implication tracing:

- Starts from an observable present reality.
- Preserves evidence at each step.
- Separates observation from inference.
- Identifies weak links and assumptions.
- Survives contradiction review.
- Produces economically relevant implications without requiring a trade recommendation.

The objective is not to prove that any particular implication will occur. The objective is to test whether the tracing method improves research quality enough to be useful inside the Investment Thesis Factory.

## 4. Why Claim 003 Matters

If CLAIM_003 is true, the factory can evaluate present realities by mapping how they may affect customers, competitors, suppliers, cash flows, market structure, asset pricing, capital allocation, or value capture.

If CLAIM_003 is false, the factory risks converting present realities into unsupported narratives. In that case, candidate quality may depend on analyst storytelling rather than repeatable evidence review.

CLAIM_003 matters because consequence tracing is the bridge between observing a reality and deciding whether that reality may become a valid Discovery Candidate.

## 5. Null Hypothesis

The validation program begins with this null hypothesis:

- Implications of present realities cannot be traced systematically with sufficient reliability.
- Consequence chains are primarily narrative constructions.
- Different reviewers will produce materially different implication paths from the same evidence.
- Later outcomes are required to determine which implications mattered.

The burden of proof is on CLAIM_003, not on the null hypothesis.

## 6. Evidence Required

Validation requires:

- Contemporaneous evidence for the present reality.
- Step-level evidence for each implication.
- Contradictory evidence for each major link.
- Alternative explanations for observed relationships.
- Reviewer notes distinguishing evidence from inference.
- Records of weak links, assumptions, and uncertainty.
- Comparison against cases where consequence tracing fails.

Evidence must be specific enough for later reviewers to reconstruct why each implication was accepted, weakened, rejected, or left unresolved.

## 8. Consequence Chain Requirements

Each consequence chain must document:

- Present reality.
- First-order implications.
- Second-order implications.
- Possible economic effects.
- Potential value creation.
- Potential value capture.
- Possible investment relevance.
- Weak links.
- Assumptions.
- Contradictions.
- Alternative explanations.

Each link must be reviewable independently. A plausible final implication cannot validate unsupported intermediate links.

## 9. Systematic Tracing Test

The tracing method must answer:

- Can the same present reality generate a structured implication chain under the same rules?
- Are implication steps explicit rather than implied?
- Are evidence and inference separated?
- Are weak links visible before conclusions are formed?
- Can another reviewer audit the path without relying on memory?

The test fails when implication chains depend mainly on unstated intuition, retrospective coherence, or broad thematic language.

## 10. Reviewer Reproducibility Test

CLAIM_003 requires evidence that different reviewers can apply the tracing process consistently.

The test must compare:

- Whether reviewers identify the same present reality.
- Whether reviewers identify materially similar first-order implications.
- Whether reviewers identify similar weak links.
- Whether reviewers assign similar evidence quality concerns.
- Whether disagreements are explainable through documented evidence rather than preference.

Perfect agreement is not required. Unexplained divergence is evidence against systematic traceability.

## 11. Evidence Boundary Rules

Every case must establish an evidence boundary before tracing begins.

Rules:

- Define the discovery date before analysis.
- Use only information available at the discovery date.
- Do not use later outcomes to select the consequence path.
- Do not upgrade link confidence because the outcome later occurred.
- Do not downgrade contradictions because the final narrative is attractive.

Cases that cannot preserve evidence boundaries must be marked compromised and cannot provide strong support for CLAIM_003.

## 12. Disconfirmation Criteria

CLAIM_003 is disconfirmed or materially weakened when evidence shows:

- Consequence chains require hindsight to become coherent.
- Reviewers cannot reproduce similar implication paths from the same evidence.
- Key links are unsupported by evidence.
- Alternative explanations explain the implication path better.
- Contradictions exceed support at important links.
- Implications are too generic to improve discovery quality.
- Chains collapse before reaching economic relevance.
- Value capture cannot be separated from speculative forecasting.

Disconfirming evidence must be preserved. It must not be reframed as partial support without narrowing and retesting the claim.

## 13. Acceptance Criteria

CLAIM_003 may be accepted only if evidence shows:

- Present realities can generate explicit implication chains before outcomes become obvious.
- Major implication links can be supported with contemporaneous evidence.
- Contradictions and alternative explanations can be identified at link level.
- Independent reviewers can audit the chain.
- Consequence tracing improves Discovery Candidate review quality.
- The method works across more than one case type.
- The traced implications can reach economic relevance without producing investment advice.

Acceptance requires more than a compelling example. It requires evidence that the process is repeatable, auditable, and useful.

## 14. Rejection Criteria

CLAIM_003 must be rejected when evidence supports the null hypothesis.

Rejection is required if:

- Consequence tracing repeatedly produces narrative explanations rather than evidence-linked chains.
- Implication paths cannot be reproduced or audited.
- The method cannot distinguish strong chains from weak chains.
- Most chains depend on forecast assumptions rather than observable realities.
- Contradictions routinely overwhelm support.
- The process does not improve candidate review, rejection, or retirement decisions.

Rejected findings must preserve failed criteria, contradictions, alternative explanations, evidence weaknesses, and lessons learned.

## 15. Retirement Criteria

CLAIM_003 may be retired rather than rejected if the claim is no longer active but has not been invalidated.

Retirement may be appropriate when:

- Validation is deferred because prior claims remain unresolved.
- Evidence is stale but not contradictory.
- The claim is superseded by a more precise claim.
- The current plan has completed its procedural role.

Retirement requires a retirement rationale, retirement date, future reactivation conditions, evidence state, historical scores, lineage, review history, evidence history, and score history. Retirement must not be used to hide invalidation.

## 16. Audit and Evidence Requirements

Every validation artifact must preserve:

- Claim ID.
- Review date.
- Discovery date for each case.
- Evidence boundary.
- Source evidence.
- Consequence chain.
- Link-level evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Alternative explanations.
- Reviewer rationale.
- Score rationale.
- Decision rationale.

Audit records must distinguish source facts, interpretation, uncertainty, contradiction, and decision. Later reviewers must be able to reconstruct the validation decision without relying on narrative memory.

## 17. Confidence Scoring

Each case must be scored from 1 to 10 across:

- Present Reality Clarity.
- Link Evidence Quality.
- Consequence Chain Coherence.
- Contradiction Control.
- Alternative Explanation Control.
- Reviewer Reproducibility.
- Economic Relevance.
- Forecast Independence.
- Auditability.
- Overall Confidence.

Scores require written rationale. Overall Confidence must not exceed Link Evidence Quality or Auditability. High narrative coherence without evidence must receive a low score.

## 18. Decision Framework

Possible outcomes:

- Validated.
- Partially Validated.
- Inconclusive.
- Rejected.
- Retired.

Validated requires repeatable evidence that consequence tracing works across cases, survives contradiction review, and improves candidate quality.

Partially Validated means the process works under narrower conditions that must be stated explicitly.

Inconclusive means evidence is insufficient, mixed, stale, or compromised by hindsight controls.

Rejected means the evidence favors the null hypothesis.

Retired means validation is no longer active without a finding that the claim is invalid.

## 19. Explicit Non-Goals

This validation plan does not authorize:

- Investment advice.
- Trade recommendations.
- Security selection.
- Buy, sell, or hold conclusions.
- Portfolio construction.
- Capital allocation.
- Manual trade capture.
- Automated execution.
- Discovery Candidate creation.
- Discovery Candidate promotion.
- Candidate registry expansion.
- New dashboards, APIs, databases, engines, workflows, or automation systems.

The plan defines standards for research validation only.
