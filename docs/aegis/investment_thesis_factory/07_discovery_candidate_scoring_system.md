# Discovery Candidate Scoring System

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the scoring methodology used to evaluate Discovery Candidates consistently across all future research.

The objective is not to prove that candidates are attractive. The objective is to rank, compare, challenge, and eliminate candidates using a repeatable framework. Every Discovery Candidate begins with zero credibility and must earn confidence through evidence.

## 2. Design Principles

The scoring system must be:

- Repeatable.
- Auditable.
- Evidence-based.
- Hostile to narratives.
- Comparable across candidates.
- Independent of stock performance.

Scores must support research discipline, not persuasion.

## 3. Scoring Philosophy

Scores measure research quality, not expected investment return.

Scores do not imply attractiveness.

Scores do not imply investment action.

Scoring exists to expose weak candidates, inconsistent reasoning, unsupported assumptions, and excessive narrative confidence.

## 4. Core Scoring Dimensions

Each Discovery Candidate must be scored from 1 to 10 across the following dimensions:

- Truth.
- Importance.
- Underappreciation.
- Investability.
- Evidence Quality.
- Consequence Depth.
- Contradiction Strength.

Scores must be supported by written rationale. Unsupported scores are invalid.

## 5. Truth Score

The Truth Score measures how likely the proposed reality is to be true.

Required questions:

- How likely is the reality to be true?
- How strong is the evidence?
- How much contradictory evidence exists?

A high Truth Score requires direct evidence, independent confirmation where available, and limited unresolved contradiction.

## 6. Importance Score

The Importance Score measures the economic significance of the proposed reality.

Required questions:

- How economically significant is the reality?
- How large is the potential impact?
- How broad is the effect?

A high Importance Score requires a credible path from the observed reality to meaningful economic consequences.

## 7. Underappreciation Score

The Underappreciation Score measures whether the reality or its implications appear insufficiently understood.

Required questions:

- How widely understood is the reality?
- How widely understood are the implications?
- How crowded is the discovery space?

A high Underappreciation Score requires evidence that the reality or its implications are not broadly reflected in conventional research, financial media, sell-side coverage, or common investor narratives.

## 8. Investability Score

The Investability Score measures whether the reality can plausibly connect to investable consequences.

Required questions:

- Can economic value be captured?
- Can beneficiaries be identified?
- Can consequences plausibly affect investment outcomes?

A high Investability Score requires an identifiable value capture path. It does not require a stock recommendation, trade idea, valuation opinion, or portfolio decision.

## 9. Evidence Quality Score

The Evidence Quality Score measures the reliability and decision-usefulness of the evidence base.

Required questions:

- How reliable is the evidence?
- How diverse are the sources?
- How dependent is the candidate on assumptions?

A high Evidence Quality Score requires relevant, current, specific, and reviewable evidence. Assumption-heavy candidates must score lower.

## 10. Consequence Depth Score

The Consequence Depth Score measures the strength and depth of the candidate's consequence chain.

Required questions:

- How many credible consequence layers exist?
- How strong are the consequence links?
- How plausible is the economic chain?

A high Consequence Depth Score requires a coherent chain from present reality to first-order consequences, second-order consequences, economic impact, and potential investment relevance.

## 11. Contradiction Strength Score

The Contradiction Strength Score measures the force of evidence against the candidate.

Required questions:

- How strong are the competing explanations?
- How vulnerable is the candidate?
- How much unresolved contradiction exists?

Lower contradiction strength should improve confidence.

Higher contradiction strength should reduce confidence.

The score must not be inverted or ignored. Strong contradiction is evidence against promotion.

## 12. Overall Confidence Score

The Overall Confidence Score summarizes the current research confidence in the candidate.

It must incorporate:

- Supporting evidence.
- Contradictions.
- Evidence quality.
- Falsifiability.

Overall Confidence must not exceed the quality of the underlying evidence. A candidate with weak evidence, weak falsifiability, or unresolved contradiction cannot receive a high confidence score.

## 13. Discovery Quality Index (DQI)

Discovery Quality Index is a composite measure of discovery quality independent of investment return.

DQI must use an explicit formula structure that combines:

- Positive evidence dimensions.
- Consequence quality.
- Investability.
- Underappreciation.
- Evidence quality.
- Contradiction penalty.
- Falsifiability assessment.

Weights must not be hardcoded in this standard. DQI requires future empirical calibration using historical validation, prospective validation, and reviewer calibration data.

## 14. Promotion Thresholds

Promotion requires passing thresholds across multiple dimensions. A single high score cannot compensate for failure in a core dimension.

Candidate may advance to Under Review when:

- The proposed reality is defined clearly.
- Initial evidence exists.
- The candidate is falsifiable.
- The candidate can undergo contradiction review.

Under Review may advance to Supported Candidate when:

- Truth, Importance, Underappreciation, and Investability all pass minimum review thresholds.
- Evidence Quality is sufficient for independent review.
- Contradictions are documented.
- Consequence Depth is plausible.

Supported Candidate may advance to Promoted Candidate when:

- Evidence exceeds contradiction.
- The consequence chain is economically meaningful.
- Potential value capture is identified.
- Overall Confidence is justified.
- DQI is above the promotion threshold set by future governance.

## 15. Rejection Thresholds

Automatic rejection is required when:

- Truth is too low.
- Importance is too low.
- Investability is too low.
- Evidence quality is insufficient.
- Contradictions overwhelm evidence.

Candidates may also be rejected when underappreciation cannot be shown, consequence chains cannot be mapped, or scoring depends on hindsight.

## 16. Scoring Governance

Scoring rules:

- Scores require justification.
- Scores must be documented.
- Scores are reviewable.
- Scores may be challenged.

Score changes must preserve reviewer rationale, date of change, and evidence basis. Governance must treat score inflation as a research failure.

## 17. Score Degradation Rules

Scores must be reduced when:

- Contradictions increase.
- Evidence weakens.
- Alternative explanations strengthen.
- New information emerges.

Score degradation is mandatory when the original basis for confidence no longer holds. Candidates must not retain high scores because they were previously promoted.

## 18. Reviewer Calibration Requirements

Reviewer calibration reviews must occur periodically.

Calibration must:

- Compare scores across candidates.
- Identify score inflation.
- Identify narrative-driven scoring.
- Test whether weak and strong candidates are being distinguished.
- Review whether contradiction penalties are being applied consistently.

Calibration results must be documented and used to update scoring guidance when needed.

## 19. Outputs

This scoring system produces:

- Discovery Candidate Scorecards.
- DQI Reports.
- Promotion Decisions.
- Rejection Decisions.
- Scoring Audit Reports.

Outputs must make the evidence basis, score rationale, contradiction treatment, and final status auditable.
