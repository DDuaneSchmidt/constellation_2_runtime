# Discovery Validation Standard

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the evidence standard, validation program, scoring framework, and failure conditions required to test whether the AEGIS Investment Thesis Factory can systematically discover economically important, underappreciated, investable realities.

The factory is unproven until evidence demonstrates otherwise. Its claims must be tested against historical cases, prospective discovery work, and comparison groups without stock recommendations, portfolio construction, trade generation, or implementation design.

## 2. Core Claim Under Test

AEGIS can systematically discover economically important realities before their implications are broadly appreciated.

This claim is not accepted by assertion. It is accepted only if repeatable evidence shows that the discovery process identifies true, important, underappreciated, investable realities in a way that is distinguishable from ordinary research, media coverage, sell-side framing, and common investor narratives.

## 3. Claim Hierarchy

CLAIM_001: Recurring opportunity archetypes exist.

CLAIM_002: Important investment opportunities often originate from underappreciated present realities.

CLAIM_003: The implications of present realities can be traced systematically.

CLAIM_004: A systematic process can discover those realities better than conventional fundamental research.

Each claim must be validated separately. Later claims cannot be treated as proven because earlier claims appear plausible.

## 4. Discovery Candidate Definition

A Discovery Candidate is:

A potentially important, true, underappreciated, investable reality.

A candidate is not a thesis, recommendation, trade, or portfolio input. It is a falsifiable research object that may or may not lead to an investable consequence chain.

## 5. Four-Filter Discovery Model

Every Discovery Candidate must pass four filters:

- Truth: The reality must be supported by evidence available at the time of discovery.
- Importance: The reality must plausibly affect economic behavior, cash flows, market structure, competitive position, asset pricing, or capital allocation.
- Underappreciation: The reality or its implications must be materially absent, minimized, misframed, or misunderstood in common narratives.
- Investability: The reality must plausibly connect to publicly observable assets, sectors, instruments, or economic exposures without requiring a stock recommendation.

Failure on any filter prevents the candidate from being treated as validated.

## 6. Validation Program Structure

The validation program has three phases:

- Phase 1: Historical Discovery Validation.
- Phase 2: Prospective Discovery Validation.
- Phase 3: Comparative Discovery Validation.

Each phase must use predeclared criteria, candidate records, contradiction review, and scoring. Discovery success and investment success must be measured separately.

## 7. Phase 1 — Historical Discovery Validation

Phase 1 tests whether the process could have identified historical Discovery Candidates without hindsight bias.

Historical cases must be reconstructed from time-bounded evidence. The case file must define what was knowable at the decision date, what was not yet knowable, and what conventional narratives emphasized at that time.

Historical validation must not explain past winners by importing later facts. A case only counts if the candidate, evidence, underappreciation claim, and consequence chain could have been stated before broad recognition occurred.

## 8. Phase 2 — Prospective Discovery Validation

Phase 2 generates current Discovery Candidates without producing stock recommendations.

Each prospective candidate must include a timestamped statement of the reality, evidence basis, underappreciation claim, possible consequence chain, contradiction review, falsification criteria, and observation plan.

The output of Phase 2 is a research queue, not a trading queue. It must not identify buy, sell, hold, sizing, timing, portfolio, or execution actions.

## 9. Phase 3 — Comparative Discovery Validation

Phase 3 compares AEGIS discoveries against:

- Conventional research.
- Financial media.
- Sell-side coverage.
- Common investor narratives.

The comparison must test whether AEGIS identifies different realities, earlier implications, deeper consequence chains, or stronger falsifiable candidates than the comparison set. A candidate is not novel because it uses different wording; it must differ in substance, timing, implication, or evidence synthesis.

## 10. Case Study Template

Each case study must include:

- Candidate identifier.
- Discovery date or historical reconstruction date.
- Candidate statement.
- Evidence available at the time.
- Truth assessment.
- Importance assessment.
- Underappreciation assessment.
- Investability assessment.
- Conventional narrative at the time.
- Contradiction review.
- Falsification criteria.
- Consequence chain.
- Scoring record.
- Outcome review.
- Discovery success assessment.
- Investment success assessment, if applicable and separately measured.

Historical case studies must use no hindsight language.

## 11. Discovery Candidate Scoring Framework

Each Discovery Candidate is scored on a 0 to 5 scale for each dimension:

- Truth Score: Evidence quality, source reliability, directness, and falsifiability.
- Importance Score: Magnitude of possible economic consequence.
- Underappreciation Score: Degree to which the reality or its implications are absent, mispriced, ignored, minimized, or misframed.
- Investability Score: Clarity of connection to investable exposures without requiring a recommendation.
- Novelty Score: Substantive difference from conventional research, media, sell-side coverage, and common narratives.
- Consequence Depth Score: Number, quality, and traceability of implication steps from reality to economic consequence.

Scores must include written justification. Unsupported scores are invalid.

## 12. Discovery Quality Index

The Discovery Quality Index is a composite score that measures discovery quality independent of investment return.

The index should evaluate whether a candidate was true, important, underappreciated, investable, novel, and capable of generating a traceable consequence chain. It must not be retroactively upgraded because an associated asset performed well, and it must not be automatically downgraded because an associated asset performed poorly.

Investment return may be reviewed separately, but it cannot substitute for discovery quality.

## 13. Failure Conditions

The validation program fails if any of the following are observed:

- No recurring archetypes found.
- Discovery candidates are mostly obvious.
- Discovery process is no better than conventional research.
- Discovery candidates rarely lead to investable consequence chains.
- Scoring cannot distinguish strong candidates from weak candidates.
- Historical validation relies on hindsight explanations.

Failure must be recorded directly. It must not be reframed as partial success unless the remaining claim is explicitly narrowed and retested.

## 14. Governance Rules

The factory must follow these rules:

- No stock recommendations.
- No portfolio construction.
- No trade generation.
- No implementation design.
- No hindsight language in historical cases.
- Every candidate must be falsifiable.
- Every candidate must include contradiction review.
- Discovery success and investment success must be measured separately.

Any output that violates these rules is outside this standard.

## 15. Research Output Standard

Research output must be concise, timestamped, falsifiable, and evidence-linked. Each output must distinguish observed reality, inferred implication, uncertainty, contradiction, and next validation step.

A valid output must not claim that AEGIS has discovered a durable edge unless the validation program supports that claim. The default posture is skeptical: candidates are unvalidated until evidence accumulates.
