# Discovery Candidate Review Protocol

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines the mandatory review process applied to every Discovery Candidate before promotion, rejection, retirement, or escalation.

The purpose of review is not to support candidates. The purpose of review is to challenge candidates. Every candidate is assumed wrong until sufficient evidence survives hostile review.

## 2. Review Philosophy

Review exists to identify weaknesses.

Review exists to identify missing evidence.

Review exists to identify contradictory evidence.

Review exists to challenge assumptions.

Review is not an advocacy process.

## 3. Review Objectives

Every review must evaluate:

- Truth
- Importance
- Underappreciation
- Investability
- Evidence quality
- Consequence depth
- Value capture plausibility
- Contradiction strength

The review must separate evidence from interpretation and must not treat narrative coherence as evidence.

## 4. Review Triggers

A Discovery Candidate must be reviewed when:

- Candidate created
- Candidate promoted
- Material evidence changes
- Contradictions increase
- Scores change materially
- Retirement proposed
- Rejection proposed

Review may also be triggered when prior assumptions become stale, unclear, or insufficiently documented.

## 5. Review Participants

Required review roles:

- Primary Researcher
- Hostile Reviewer
- Evidence Reviewer
- Registry Reviewer

The Primary Researcher documents the candidate and evidence. The Hostile Reviewer challenges the thesis, assumptions, and consequence chain. The Evidence Reviewer evaluates source reliability and evidence sufficiency. The Registry Reviewer verifies status, scoring, lineage, and auditability.

Future expansion of review roles is allowed when additional controls are required.

## 6. Review Inputs

Every review must use:

- Discovery Candidate Registry
- Scorecard
- Evidence References
- Contradiction References
- Prior Reviews
- Status History

Missing inputs must be documented before review conclusions are accepted.

## 7. Stage 1 — Evidence Review

The review must answer:

- What evidence exists?
- How reliable is it?
- What assumptions are embedded?
- What evidence is missing?

Evidence quality must be assessed before candidate plausibility is reassessed.

## 8. Stage 2 — Contradiction Review

The review must answer:

- What evidence argues against the candidate?
- What evidence weakens confidence?
- What evidence would invalidate the candidate?

Contradictory evidence must remain visible even if reviewers believe it has been addressed.

## 9. Stage 3 — Alternative Explanation Review

The review must answer:

- What other explanations exist?
- Could the evidence support another interpretation?
- Why is the current interpretation preferred?

Alternative explanations must be documented as competing interpretations, not dismissed by assertion.

## 10. Stage 4 — Four-Filter Reassessment

The review must reassess:

- Truth
- Importance
- Underappreciation
- Investability

Failure in any filter must be documented and may justify rejection, escalation, or continued review.

## 11. Stage 5 — Consequence Chain Review

The review must answer:

- Does the consequence chain remain plausible?
- Where are weak links?
- Where are unsupported assumptions?

The consequence chain must not rely on unstated assumptions, later outcomes, or desired investment relevance.

## 12. Stage 6 — Value Capture Review

The review must answer:

- Who captures value?
- Why?
- Could value leak elsewhere?
- Is investability still plausible?

Value creation and value capture must be evaluated separately.

## 13. Stage 7 — Scoring Reassessment

Every review must include documented reassessment of all candidate scores.

Every score change must include justification. Score changes must identify whether the driver was new evidence, weakened evidence, stronger contradiction, alternative explanation, or reviewer calibration.

## 14. Stage 8 — Status Recommendation

Permitted review outcomes:

- Maintain Status
- Promote
- Reject
- Retire
- Escalate For Additional Research

The recommendation must be supported by evidence, contradiction review, and score rationale.

## 15. Promotion Review Requirements

Promotion requires:

- Evidence exceeds contradiction
- Four-filter pass
- Value capture plausibility
- Documented rationale

Promotion does not imply a stock recommendation, trade idea, portfolio decision, or investment action.

## 16. Rejection Review Requirements

Rejection requires documentation of:

- Failed criteria
- Key contradictions
- Alternative explanations
- Lessons learned

Rejected candidates must remain preserved in the registry.

## 17. Retirement Review Requirements

Retirement requires:

- Reason for retirement
- Future reactivation conditions
- Registry preservation

Retirement means the candidate is no longer active. It does not necessarily mean the candidate was invalidated.

## 18. Reviewer Challenge Requirements

Every review must explicitly answer:

- Why might this candidate be wrong?
- What is the strongest contradictory evidence?
- What would change our minds?
- What assumption is most fragile?

Answers must be specific enough to support later audit.

## 19. Review Documentation Requirements

Every review must preserve:

- Review date
- Reviewers
- Inputs
- Findings
- Score changes
- Status recommendation
- Rationale

Documentation must distinguish evidence, interpretation, contradiction, and decision.

## 20. Governance Rules

- No promotion without contradiction review.
- No rejection without rationale.
- No undocumented score changes.
- No deletion of review history.
- No narrative-only decisions.

Discovery review and investment action remain separate stages.

## 21. Outputs

Required outputs:

- Review Report
- Updated Scorecard
- Status Recommendation
- Contradiction Log
- Review Audit Record

Outputs must be suitable for later audit, reviewer calibration, and registry reconciliation.
