# Cheap Experiment Principles

Atlas V2 treats expensive attention as scarce and cheap experimentation as abundant.

Cheap experiments are small prediction-to-outcome cycles used to collect learning evidence. They do not create ideas, hypotheses, candidates, sleeves, paper positions, trades, recommendations, allocations, or validation authority.

Principles:

- Optimize for outcome-linked experience, not activity volume.
- Prefer many low-cost sanity cycles before any expensive attention allocation.
- Keep every experiment record/read/audit only.
- Preserve rejected and inconclusive experiments as learning evidence.
- Unknown outcomes remain distinct from failed outcomes.
- Cheap experiments may create ExperienceEvent records only when linked to a prediction and observed outcome.
- Learning evidence can inform future behavior, but it cannot validate an investment claim.
